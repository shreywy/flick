A proper player for the films on your drive.

Flick reads your Movies and TV Shows folders, fetches the artwork, sorts new downloads and plays nearly any file, with seeking that lands on the exact second. Open it, watch something, close it. No server, no account.

View the code See it move
Motion

The card you click becomes the player.

Picking something flies its thumbnail to the middle of the screen and opens the player out of it. Going back runs the same flight in reverse and drops it into its slot. Play buttons grow straight out to the edges. All of it is the browser's View Transitions API, so the page underneath is the real one, not a copy.

Features

Everything a streaming app does, for files you already have.

Library

Real artwork, saved next to the files

Posters, backdrops, title logos, cast and episode stills come from TMDB and are written into each title's folder, so they're fetched once and show up in other players too. Marvel, Star Wars and anime collections are built from the metadata.

Sorting

New downloads put themselves away

Anything that lands in the downloads folder is renamed, moved into Movies or TV Shows, and given art and an English subtitle when Flick opens. Files still being written are left alone until they stop changing, and names it can't read wait in Settings with a Fix match button.

Player

Plays nearly anything and seeks exactly

MP4s with AAC audio play as they are. Everything else goes through ffmpeg: the video is copied untouched and DTS, AC3, TrueHD or 5.1 Opus audio is converted on the fly. Styled anime subtitles render through libass, the engine mpv uses.

Subtitles

Every part of the subtitles is adjustable

Size by the pixel, font, text and background colour, opacity, outline, shadow, height and delay, all changed live over the film. Missing subtitles can be searched for and downloaded from OpenSubtitles or SubDL without leaving the player.

Skip intro

Finds intros and credits by listening

Flick fingerprints the audio of an episode and the episodes either side of it with Chromaprint, then looks for the longest stretch they share near the start and the end. An intro only counts when both neighbours agree, so last week's recap isn't mistaken for one. When the credits start, the next episode card comes up.

Ultrawide

Made on a 21:9 screen

Fill finds the black bars burned into a film with ffmpeg's cropdetect and scales until the picture meets the edges, without stretching it. Ctrl + scroll zooms by hand, and the choice is remembered per film.

Seek previews

Thumbnails along the seek bar, cut from keyframes in a single ffmpeg pass and cached.

Search as you type

Matches titles, cast, directors and collections, with the matching part highlighted.

A menu for what you clicked

Right-click a card to play from the start, mark it watched, or take it out of Continue watching, with Undo.

How playback works

Exact seeking on a stream that's being converted.

A piped ffmpeg stream can't normally be seeked: the browser sees a file with no length that starts at zero. Flick starts ffmpeg at the nearest keyframe, appends the fragments to a Media Source buffer placed at that point in the film, and lets the video element's own clock do the rest. Seeks inside what's buffered are instant; seeks outside start a new stream in about a second and a half.

Run it

Three commands.

# Node 24 and ffmpeg (a build with Chromaprint, like gyan.dev's full build)
git clone https://github.com/shreywy/flick
cd flick && npm install
npm start          # or npm run dist for release\win-unpacked\Flick.exe
